FOR CHURCHES AND GROUPS

Free to use and share.

I built this course because I wanted something that could help everyday Christians work through the basic case for God and Christianity without needing a background in philosophy, theology, or apologetics.

I also did not want it to just be a list of arguments to memorize. I want people to understand why the arguments work, how the different pieces fit together, and how they might actually explain them in a real conversation.

If you are a pastor, elder, teacher, parent, small-group leader, or you are discipling someone one-on-one, feel free to use the course however it is helpful.

You do not need to ask permission to share the site or any of the individual lessons.

How could you use it?

Someone can work through the course completely on their own, but it could also work well for a Sunday school class, Bible study, youth or young-adult group, homeschool, Christian school, family study, or one-on-one discipleship.

For a group, you could simply have everyone complete a lesson before meeting and then talk through the argument, objections, and practice question together.

You also do not have to send someone through the whole course just because they asked one question. If someone is asking about the resurrection, share the resurrection lesson. If they are struggling with the problem of evil, share that lesson. If they are asking why Christians believe God exists, there are several lessons dealing with that part of the case.

What is actually in the course?

The course follows the basic progression of classical apologetics.

It starts with logic, truth, evidence, and how arguments work. Then it works through the case for the existence of God, including causation, the beginning of the universe, design, fine-tuning, morality, and reason.

Once that case is established, the course moves to the next question: has God actually revealed himself?

That part looks at Jesus historically, his claims, his death and resurrection, his authority, and then Scripture.

There are also bonus lessons on how to begin an apologetics conversation, the problem of evil and suffering, and creation, evolution, and worldview.

Each lesson has teaching, evidence, a short comprehension question, common objections, and a practice question. There is also a Practice Lab for working through harder conversations.

Where should someone start?

If someone is new to apologetics, I would recommend starting with Study 1 and working through the studies in order. They build on each other.

There is also an optional pre-test if someone wants a baseline before starting.

If a person is already dealing with one particular question, though, it is completely fine to start with that lesson and go from there.

A few practical things

The course is free.

There is no account, subscription, or paywall.

Progress is saved in the learner's browser. The full course also includes a pre-test, post-test, progress tracking, and the Practice Lab.

If you think it would be useful for someone in your church, family, school, or group, feel free to use it and share it.
