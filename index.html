WHAT IS CLASSICAL APOLOGETICS?

Classical apologetics is a two-step way of defending Christianity: first ask what reason and the world itself tell us about God, then ask whether the historical evidence points to God revealing himself in Jesus Christ. Studying it helps you understand why you believe, recognize where a real disagreement begins, answer common objections, and explain the case in ordinary language to someone who may not already accept the Bible. The course follows one investigation from beginning to end: what best explains the world we actually have, and if that evidence points to God, has this God acted in history through Jesus Christ?

A NOTE ON CLASSICAL APOLOGETICS

The arguments fit together, but none of them does everything.

Broadly speaking, classical apologetics makes a case for God and then a separate historical case for Christianity. Different apologists build that case differently. Here, each argument has to stand on its own evidence and stay within its own conclusion.

Do not skip steps

A cosmological argument may support a necessary foundation. You still need further reasons before talking about the Trinity or the resurrection.

History is usually cumulative

Historical claims are usually supported by several lines of evidence taken together, not one knockdown proof.

An objection has to be answered, not feared

A good objection may expose a weak premise or force you to qualify a claim. That is useful. It tells you where the real work is.
