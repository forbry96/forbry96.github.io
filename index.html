WHAT IS CLASSICAL APOLOGETICS?

Classical apologetics is pretty simple in its basic structure. First, is there good reason to believe God exists? If there is, the next question is whether that God has actually revealed himself in Jesus Christ. That is the path I use here. You do not need to force every conversation through the whole course. The point is to know where you are in the case, what evidence belongs there, and what still has to be shown.

THE CLASSICAL APOLOGETICS PATH

Work through the case in order, or go straight to the question you need.

The first 20 studies make up the core course. Studies 1–2 cover logic and truth. Studies 3–9 work through the case for God. Study 10 deals with whether miracles can be ruled out if God exists. Studies 11–20 move into Jesus, the resurrection, his authority, and Scripture. Then there are three bonus studies on real conversations, evil and suffering, and creation, evolution, and worldview. Each study teaches one part of the case, shows the evidence I think matters, and gives you a chance to explain it yourself.

PRACTICE LAB

Practice the conversation, not just the answer.

Start with Guided if you are still learning where the arguments fit. Move up when that starts getting easy. In Pressure and Mixed, I made the wrong answers sound more reasonable on purpose. Real conversations do not give you four choices with one ridiculous answer. The goal is to listen, figure out what the person is actually saying, and answer that.

WHERE DO YOU BEGIN?

Figure out what the real disagreement is.

Do not see a word like “science” or “morality” and jump to a memorized answer. Figure out what the person is actually claiming and whether you need to ask a question first.

BRANCHING PRACTICE

See what happens when the conversation pushes back.

Some answers will sound pretty good. You will not see how they are graded until the end. Sometimes the better move is a question, a fair concession, or admitting that you stated something too strongly.

YOUR OWN WORDS

Now answer it without the choices.

Answer the opening claim and two follow-ups before you see a model. Then compare your answer. Did you answer the real issue? Did you use the right evidence? Did you claim more than the argument can show? Could the other person actually respond to you?

WHEN THE CONVERSATION DRIFTS

Keep the actual question on the table.

Conversations wander. These are some common ways the discussion can move away from the question you were actually talking about. Sometimes the best thing to do is just point that out and come back to it.

A NOTE ON CLASSICAL APOLOGETICS

These arguments work together, but do not make one argument do too much.

The basic approach here is to make the case for God first, then make the historical case for Christianity. Different apologists arrange things differently. I try to keep each argument doing only the work it can actually do instead of making one argument prove more than it proves.

Do not skip steps

A cosmological argument may support a necessary foundation. You still need further reasons before talking about the Trinity or the resurrection.

History usually works as a cumulative case

With history, one fact rarely settles everything. Several lines of evidence matter together.

Let objections do their job

If an objection exposes a weak premise or makes you qualify what you said, that is useful. Fix the argument instead of pretending the objection does not matter.
