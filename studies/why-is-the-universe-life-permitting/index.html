Why is the universe life-permitting?

The basic settings of the universe allow stars, chemistry, and life. Why are they in the right range?

A WALK WITH JORDAN

Looking up

The walk runs later than usual, and by the time you turn back the stars are coming out. Jordan looks up for a while and says, “I will give you this, it is strange that any of this works at all.”

You mention that the basic conditions of the universe have to fall within particular ranges for stable matter, stars, chemistry, and life to be possible.

Jordan says, “But of course we find ourselves in a universe where life can exist. If it could not, we would not be here talking about it.”

You ask whether that tells you why those conditions exist in the first place. He pauses. “No, I guess not. So what are the actual options?”

THE IDEA

The laws, constants, and initial conditions of the universe fall within life-permitting ranges. The main explanatory options are necessity, chance, or design.

Fine-tuning takes the general design argument and gives us something more specific to examine. The question is why the basic physical setup of the universe falls within ranges that allow stable matter, stars, chemistry, and eventually embodied life.

THE EVIDENCE

What exactly is fine-tuned?

Fine-tuning can sound more complicated than it really is. Physics describes the universe using basic constants, quantities, and initial conditions. The important point is that many of these cannot vary very far before you lose things that complex life depends on, such as stable atoms, long-lived stars, and useful chemistry.

This does not mean the entire universe is comfortable for life. Most of it clearly is not. The point is that the basic physical structure of the universe allows complex embodied life to exist anywhere at all.

Once we recognize that, we have to ask why the universe has that kind of setup. One possibility is necessity, meaning the constants could not have been different. Another is chance. A multiverse is one version of that response, because if there are enough universes with different values, then it becomes less surprising that at least one permits observers. A third possibility is design.

Treating the fact that we are here as a complete answer misses something: of course observers can only observe a universe that allows observers. That tells us why we should not be surprised to find ourselves in a life-permitting universe if one exists. It does not explain why life-permitting conditions exist in the first place.

Fine-tuning does not force someone to accept design by itself, but design still deserves serious consideration. A universe with very specific life-permitting conditions makes good sense if mind and purpose are part of the ultimate explanation of reality.

Bottom line: Fine-tuning asks why the universe has the sensitive physical conditions needed for complex life, and design is a serious explanation.

Think about it: Why does saying, “Of course we observe a life-permitting universe, because otherwise we would not be here,” not fully explain fine-tuning?
Answer: It explains why we should not be surprised to find ourselves in a life-permitting universe once one exists. It does not explain why life-permitting conditions exist in the first place. That is still the question the fine-tuning argument is asking.
BACK ON THE WALK

Fine-tuning still needs an explanation

You grant Jordan’s observer point. Of course we could only find ourselves in a universe compatible with observers. But that does not explain why such conditions exist.

So the real discussion is between explanations such as necessity, chance, a multiverse, and design.

Jordan looks back at the sky. “I am not ready to jump straight to design, but I can see why saying ‘we are here’ does not answer the question.”

COMMON OBJECTIONS

Questions this argument still has to face

“A multiverse explains it.”

A multiverse is a real alternative and should be considered rather than dismissed. But it still needs evidence, and we still have to ask what produces the different universes and whether the model actually explains the fine-tuning better than design does.

“Fine-tuning assumes that life was the purpose of the universe.”

The argument does not have to assume that life is the only purpose of the universe. The point is simply that complex embodied life requires a restricted set of physical conditions. We can acknowledge that fact and still ask what best explains it.

KEEP THE CLAIM CLEAR

Avoid dramatic probability claims that are harder to defend. The point is that life depends on sensitive physical conditions, and we still have to compare the possible explanations.
